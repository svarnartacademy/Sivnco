## 01 · Desi Energy Bar — Structured Chaos

### Public copy

**Card description:** A character-led identity for a traditional snack, developed across illustration, wrapper design and retail packaging.

**Project credit:** Concept development, art direction, illustration, packaging design and production coordination — H P Shivaraj.

**Context:** Jus’Amazin · Commercial packaging project · Development and production across 2024–2026.

### A familiar snack with a different visual energy

For Desi Energy Bar, I developed a visual direction that brought Indian childhood references into contemporary snack packaging. The work connected a bold blue field with hand-drawn characters and familiar street-play imagery, giving the product a recognisable personality across its wrapper and counter display.

### Finding the direction

The project included a 25-store Bengaluru retail observation study and exploration of alternative packaging directions. The selected approach, Structured Chaos, combined expressive illustration with a more ordered information hierarchy. The character and colour carried the personality; the layout kept the product information legible.

### Taking the idea onto the pack

I developed the wrapper and retail dispenser artwork, then carried the design through dieline preparation, colour proofing and vendor prepress review. Working on both the creative and production stages meant checking how illustration, type and colour translated onto the physical packaging.

### What the project demonstrates

An original visual idea carried through research, illustration, packaging and commercial production. My contribution was the creative and design work and its production coordination. Product formulation, commercial approvals, distribution and sales involved the wider team.

### Private editorial notes

- Show: finished wrapper; illustration detail; genuine early explorations; wrapper/dispenser relationship; physical pack; selected non-confidential proof detail.
- Caption proposal: “Character illustration and product information brought together on the wrapper.” Use only beside a matching image.
- Keep the 6,000-pouch digital trial and 160,000-pouch rotogravure order in private evidence unless publication of quantities is cleared. An order is not proof that every unit was produced or sold.
- Sources disagree on the third rejected direction: one lists Literal Nutrition, another Illustrated Garden. Do not publish a numbered rejection narrative until original boards are checked.
- Omit timed attention claims, measured recognition percentages, shopper quotations and sole-cause sales claims.

## 04 · Universal Pouch — One World, Many Products

### Public copy

**Card description:** A shared illustrated pouch system with product-specific labels and practical alignment guides.

**Project credit:** Concept development, art direction, packaging-system design and production coordination — H P Shivaraj.

**Context:** Jus’Amazin · Commercial shared-packaging system.

### Designing the constant and the variable

The universal pouch project combined a common illustrated package with product-specific label overlays. I developed the shared visual direction and the system through which individual products could be identified within it.

The design separated what remained consistent across the range from what needed to change by product. A common pouch established the visual world; the overlays carried the changing SKU information.

### Designing for application

Pre-printed corner alignment marks helped guide label placement. These small details connected the graphic system to the practical task of applying labels on the packing floor.

### A system carried into production

I worked across the visual concept, artwork, label system and vendor proofs. Operations and packing colleagues contributed to its implementation. The result is a case about design as both visual expression and a repeatable working system.

### Private editorial notes

- Show: unlabelled common pouch → several correctly labelled products → alignment-detail diagram → physical examples.
- “Public Park Paradigm” is a name recorded in the dossier. Use it as a subtitle only if you recognise it as your original concept name.
- Keep this project distinct from the Gluten Free Atta pouch, which has a separate identity and adaptation history.
- Do not publish the 100 → 400–500 pouches/hour figure as a verified performance result: worker count and measurement method remain unresolved.
- Exclude cylinder-cost savings and procurement rates from public copy.

1. One strong final visual that actually belongs to the project.
2. Two to four details showing creative choices, not just repeated mockup angles.
3. One genuine process artefact where available: sketch, storyboard, composition study or proof.
4. A physical outcome, or an explicit “Concept / Unproduced” label.

Do not replace missing evidence with generated documentary imagery. New diagrams or reconstructed presentations must be labelled as reconstructions. Product renders must not be captioned as photographs of manufactured work.
