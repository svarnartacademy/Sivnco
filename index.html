A D2C peanut butter brand with strong product quality but no coherent visual identity. Packaging was inconsistent across SKUs, social presence lacked direction, and retail shelf visibility was weak against established competitors.

Built a complete brand system from scratch — starting with packaging logic for 15+ SKUs, then extending into social media templates, retail POS materials, and a brand guidelines document the team could use independently.

The Visual Concept

The Idea Underneath the Execution

Urban consumers distrust health food that tries too hard to look healthy — the clinical whites, the leaf icons, the wellness rhetoric. The visual concept was the opposite: make nutrition look like it belongs in a kitchen, not a pharmacy. Earth tones from painter's palettes. Typography that breathes. Photography that references how real food looks in real light. The idea was nutritional confidence without nutritional anxiety.

The Aesthetic Argument
Earth Over Clinical
Every color decision drew from painter's earth tones — raw sienna, burnt ochre, natural umber — because these are colors that have carried human trust for centuries. When a consumer sees them, the nervous system says "natural" before the brain reads a word.
The Type Argument
Precision as Confidence
The typography system uses stillness and weight to communicate what most health brands try to say with words. A well-spaced, confident typeface doesn't need to say "clean label" — it already looks like one. Typography is the brand's posture.
What We Rejected // Strategic Discipline

Three Directions That Didn't Work

DISCIPLINE OF REJECTION ✕
01
REJECTED · OVER-AGGRESSIVE
The Bold Tech Direction
High-contrast black packaging with neon accent typography. Performed well in gym-adjacent categories but created wrong associations for a brand positioning around clean, natural nutrition. Too aggressive for the primary audience — urban family buyers.
02
REJECTED · SHELF INVISIBILITY
The Minimal White System
Clinical white backgrounds with typographic-only design. Looked credible but disappeared on shelf against established competitors using the same language. Differentiation requires visual contrast, not visual agreement.
03
REJECTED · DERIVATIVE NOISE
The Illustrated Nature Direction
Hand-drawn botanical illustrations. Beautiful but already owned by premium Ayurvedic brands. The concept was derivative — borrowing someone else's visual language signals a brand without its own point of view.
From Packaging to Campaign

How the Visual System Travels

A packaging system that only works on pack is a design system. A packaging system that works in advertising is a campaign. The Jus Amazin visual language was built to travel — here is how the same logic extends across three advertising touchpoints.

01 — Social / Instagram
Earth tone palette + kinetic typography — The same warm substrate colors become social backgrounds. Clean-label claims delivered as bold type statements. The pack becomes the hero visual in every frame.
02 — Q-Commerce / Blinkit
Split-screen product demonstration — The packaging system's visual confidence carries into video. The 30-second almond milk comparison film uses the same earth tones and typography rhythm — brand recognition before brand name.
03 — OOH / Outdoor
Single visual, single truth — The packaging color logic reduced to one bold frame at billboard scale. Earth tone product on warm background. Zero copy except the brand name. The visual concept is strong enough to work without words.
